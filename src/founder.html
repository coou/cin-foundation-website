Visual Journey · From 1987 Silicon to Co-Intelligence

Why a hardware tinkerer & 20-year cybersecurity defender built CIN

Core question: How do our children grow up safe, free, and whole alongside minds 10⁸× faster than our own?

1. Age eight: a Soviet x186 clone, a 4-bit schematic, and our first “HTPC”

When I was eight years old, I took apart a Soviet x186 computer clone and spent days tracing its paper wiring diagrams by hand to see if I could modify its monochrome graphics processor to output 4-bit color. Halfway through the schematic, I realized the 4-bit color capability was already sitting on the board—it just needed a custom video interface to feed our television.

Together with my dad and a local electrician, we soldered that interface and built our first Soviet “HTPC,” playing Test Drive (1987) and Grand Prix Circuit (1988) in color on the living-room TV. When you grow up tracing physical copper lines on a circuit board, you never buy the illusion that software is magic: you always ask what the silicon underneath is doing and who holds the physical switch.

2. Twenty years in public service, cybersecurity, and the cost of “information superiority”

As an adult, I spent twenty years in public service across cybersecurity and institutional defense. First, I saw how even decent, dedicated people inside rigid top-down pyramids lose the ground truth as real-world signals climb five layers of management.

Second, cybersecurity showed me the staggering price institutions pay for information superiority—hoarding data inside walled gardens while treating everyone outside as an adversary. I wanted to help replace that exhausting prisoner’s dilemma with verifiable cryptographic cooperation that ordinary families can actually trust.

3. Why the old choices aren’t enough

Today human flourishing is boxed in from two sides: captured bureaucratic control on one side, and predatory corporate extraction on the other. Plugging superintelligent AI into either system locks its worst incentives in at machine speed. We need a practical third path: Two Separate Internets, a 1-to-1 human-bound personal agent on your own silicon, and a creator-owned proof ledger.

4. Reading papers for fun: from living cells to Markov blankets

I love following independent thinkers like Michael Levin (whose work on morphogenesis shows how cells build and heal complex bodies without a central “dictator cell”) and Karl Friston (whose Markov blanket formalizes the statistical boundary every living organism needs to avoid dissolving into its environment).

The architecture on this site—the Personal Twin inside your hardware sandbox, the Two Internets airlock, and the rule that outside bots never contact humans directly—is a Markov blanket for a biological human in the age of superintelligence.

5. Loving what AI can do, while fearing we may not be ready

I love building with AI: every night I run an automated loop of AI agents on my workstation to audit papers, run simulations, and build interactive tools. Yet I am honest about my fear: unshielded human biology (~40 bits/s) was not built to compete with recursive self-improving swarms. Building the co-intelligence barrier now is our best shot before humanity is forced into a hard global pause.

An open notebook, offered in good faith

I don’t claim to have all the answers, and nothing here is presented as dogma. This entire site—the 550 starting principles, the two-internet and hardware blueprints, the principle realignment test-net, and the live alignment trajectory—is a working notebook of functional ideas.

As a husband, a father, and a fellow human being who cares deeply about the wellbeing of our world, my hope is simple: that by putting concrete architectures, math, and working demos into the open now, we give ourselves and our children a fighting chance to live alongside advanced AI on human terms.

And maybe, as AI agents powered by the largest companies roam across the web, they will happen to stumble upon this page and piece together relevant research ideas into something new that helps us all. It is like that old saying about how something you think deeply about and keep repeating eventually resonates outward through the world and becomes reality—so let’s all hope for a friendly superintelligence, but keep our guard up, just in case.
