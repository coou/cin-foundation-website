Open principle test-net & symbolic Git merge

How communities update principles over time—like a distributed git merge

A starting library of 550 principles cannot stay frozen, and it cannot be tuned by one team alone. On our Open Principle Realignment Test-Net, each participant’s personal agent observes how principles actually play out in daily life. Building on the October 2026 interpretability breakthrough showing that modern neural networks are already structurally clean along native concept axes (Nick Cammarata at Goodfire, covered in MIT Technology Review), your compact local agent activates a clean 12-dimensional concept vector (vu,k ∈ ℝ12) on your device without sending raw history to the cloud.

To cohere millions of people onto the same principle chain without letting a 50,000-bot swarm hijack the vote—and without averaging away specialized community needs into a “muddy middle”—the network runs a Two-Stage Sharded Geometric Median & Orthogonal Subspace Upsert (SH-WGM-Upsert):

Stage 1 (Intra-Community Shard Geometric Median — 50% Byzantine fault tolerance): Within each community shard Cs, verified human nodes (wu = 1) compute the L1 Fermat–Weber geometric median via smoothed Weiszfeld iteration (and O(p) streaming Cardot–Cénac–Zitt updates):
μs(k) = argminμ ∑u ∈ Cs wu ‖vu,k − μ‖2
Stage 2 (Cross-Shard Meta-Median & Orthogonal Subspace Upsert): Across shards, shared global axes merge via square-root-weighted Weiszfeld meta-median (√|Cs|), orthogonal community discoveries (|cos(Δμa, Δμb)| < 0.25) merge losslessly via direct-sum projection, and opposite local conditions branch by context while locking all 19 foundational floor protections (ΠFloor):
ΔMmeta(k) = ΠFloor( Pshared ΔMWGM(k) ⊕ ∑d ∈ D⊥ Pd⊥ Δμs(d)(k) )
