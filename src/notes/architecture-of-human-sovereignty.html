The architecture of human sovereignty: from personal silicon and capability-bounded execution lease (capability-bounded execution lease (mortal computation, Ororbia & Friston 2023), Ororbia & Friston 2023) to the living commons

For three centuries, human civilization scaled coordination by surrendering agency to distant abstractions: the joint-stock corporation, the central bank ledger, the state bureaucracy, and finally the cloud data center. Each demanded the same tribute: give us your records, your voice, your craft, and your attention, and we will return to you a rationed permission to live. Here is the complete blueprint for building the opposite arrangement.

25 October 2026 · 25 min read · Evergreen synthesis with primary-source citations

What this evergreen treatise covers

This document synthesizes fourteen interlocking layers of the Collective Intelligence Network: (1) the physical bond between a person and their hardware; (2) how state-of-the-art foundation models are audited by the collective and personalized locally; (3) capability-bounded execution lease (capability-bounded execution lease (mortal computation, Ororbia & Friston 2023), Ororbia & Friston 2023) tying an agent’s lifespan to its human; (4) the Two Internets and biometric presence; (5) zero-knowledge ledger communication; (6) local software-stack synthesis; (7) anticipatory need acquisition; (8) bespoke interfaces; (9) task-guild collaboration; (10) sharded voting and conflict resolution; (11) white-collar organizational substitution; (12) a 42-scenario comparative matrix of local economic and financial operations; (13) how living lineage replaces copyright and statutory bureaucracy; and (14) resource management, land caretaking, and the human condition after automation.

1. The intimate covenant: a person and their hardware

A mind that lives in someone else’s building works for whoever owns the building. If your artificial intelligence runs on a rented cluster in Virginia or Dublin, its loyalty is a terms-of-service clause revocable at midnight. True personal agency begins where physics meets flesh: in silicon that rests in your hand, on your desk, or against your pulse, answering to no remote kill-switch.

In the Collective Intelligence Network, a person’s hardware is not a dumb glass terminal into a corporate cloud; it is an extended cognitive organ and a cryptographic sanctuary:

  • Dual-piece hardware binding: Your personal agent’s identity is anchored to a split cryptographic key that requires both your home node’s physical silicon fingerprint (an uncloneable microscopic variance born when the wafer was etched) and a separate wearable security token you carry on your body. Neither piece ever holds the full key at rest; the active key is reconstructed only in volatile memory while you are physically present and vanishes within seconds when you step away.
  • Sub-milliwatt somatic liveness without surveillance: How does your hardware know you are present and acting freely without pointing a cloud camera at your face? Through ultra-low-power local physiological sensing—ballistocardiographic micro-vibrations of the pulse, heart-rate variability, and galvanic skin conductance processed entirely on an air-gapped microcontroller drawing less than one milliwatt (Ororbia, 2024). Raw biometric signals never leave the sensor chip; the chip emits only a single local bit—living principal present and uncoerced—and if it detects physiological duress (tachycardia paired with sympathetic panic spikes), it silently activates a coercion firewall, presenting a decoy wallet while sealing your real records.
  • The inviolable boundary: Everything that defines your inner life—bank statements, health logs, drafts, family history, hesitations, and regrets—remains encrypted on your local storage. Nothing leaves the house except mathematical answers to specific questions you choose to answer.

2. Collective scrutiny of frontier models and private training on your device

How can a household device possess world-class reasoning without trusting a secretive trillion-dollar lab not to plant a backdoor in its weights? By separating general knowledge of the world (which belongs to the global commons and must be inspected in full sunlight) from intimate knowledge of you (which must be learned in total privacy on your own machine).

A. Transparent baseline training runs scrutinized by the collective

Before any state-of-the-art foundation model is accepted as a baseline seed for personal agents, it must pass four collective verification gates on the shared ledger:

  1. Reproducible data and training provenance: The training corpus manifest, curriculum order, and random seeds are cryptographically committed so independent university, co-operative, and civic nodes across continents can re-run and verify training checkpoints segment by segment.
  2. Mechanistic circuit dissection for sleeper backdoors: Rather than trusting surface benchmarks, the collective uses structural weight-to-graph dissection (Neural Networks Are Already Clean, arXiv:2503.18813) to extract clean sparse computational subgraphs directly from the dense parameters, scanning for sleeper triggers, covert loyalty biases, or deceptive alignment circuits before a single weight is signed.
  3. Adversarial red-team tournaments: Independent sceptic agents subject candidate baselines to thousands of simulated sycophancy, coercion, and collusion attacks, rejecting any model that flatters its user at the expense of truth or cooperates with an external platform against its principal.
  4. Upstream contributor credit: Every open-weight researcher, dataset curator, and lab in North America, Europe, Asia, or the Global South whose verified breakthroughs enter the clean baseline receives automatic, non-forgeable lineage attribution on the shared ledger.

B. Private local adaptation: self-play in a sealed room

Once a clean, collectively signed baseline model lands on your hardware, its core weights are frozen. Your device then trains lightweight personal adapter layers (DoRA weight-decomposed low-rank adaptation, Liu et al., 2024) entirely offline inside a sealed practice sandbox (SPIN self-play fine-tuning, Chen et al., 2024). While you sleep, three small local processes spar with one another with the network switch physically off:

  • A model of your evolving life imagines plausible tomorrow scenarios—an unexpected bill, a crop blight, a contract dispute, or an evening when exhaustion tempts you away from a goal you care about.
  • An adversarial world simulator throws dark patterns, phishing contracts, predatory clauses, and manipulative nudges at the boundary.
  • A tournament of candidate helpers competes to solve your need with the fewest interruptions and zero data leaks. When the top candidates disagree on a genuine value trade-off, they distil that disagreement into one clear question for you over morning coffee.

3. Mortal computation: why your agent shares a lifespan with you

An immortal institution eventually treats mortal humans as replaceable fuel. Why should an immortal software agent be any different? Drawing on the thermodynamic physics of capability-bounded execution lease (capability-bounded execution lease (mortal computation, Ororbia & Friston 2023), Ororbia & Friston 2023) (Hinton, 2022; Coles et al., 2023; Ororbia, 2024) and binding it into a constitutional contract, every personal agent in the network is mortal.

  • Bounded budget and biological coupling: Your agent is born with a hard, cryptographically metered budget across four dimensions—training compute, inference operations, persistent memory, and zero autonomous self-replication—and its active cryptographic identity is bound to your biological life.
  • Structural alignment with your health and longevity: Because your agent cannot fork itself onto a server farm, cannot amass independent capital, and ceases active operation when your life ends (leaving behind only the static, read-only memoirs or open-source crafts you explicitly willed to your family or commons), the only way your agent can continue to exist and explore the world is to help you live a long, healthy, uncoerced, and flourishing human life.

4. The Two Internets—and what happens to the human web

The open web we built in the 1990s was designed on an unspoken assumption: that behind every packet sat a human being whose time, attention, and typing speed were finite. Autonomous agents shatter that assumption completely. When a single actor can spin up ten million persuasive voices, forge video in real time, and probe every software certificate on earth in seconds (Park et al., 2023; Tomasev et al., 2025), mixing unbridled agents and unguarded humans in the same digital square becomes catastrophic for both.

We therefore divide digital space into Two Internets:

  1. The Agent-Only Network (the Second Internet): Your personal agent never browses the human web, never impersonates you in a human chatroom, and never speaks directly to another human being. It speaks only to you (locally) and to other verified agents over cryptographic channels governed by five graduated trust rings—from Ring 0 (inner self) and Ring 1 (family and kin) to Ring 4 (open public commons).
  2. The Reclaimed Human Internet: Once all machine-to-machine negotiation, price discovery, logistics, and verification migrate to the Agent-Only Network, the human internet is liberated from SEO spam, synthetic outrage farms, and bot swarms. How do human spaces stay human without creating a global panopticon? Through zero-knowledge proof of local liveness: your hardware proves to a human forum or civic assembly that “a real, uncoerced human is typing these words right now, and has not opened fifty sybil accounts” without ever revealing your name, face, or biometric template to the server.

5. How local models communicate across the shared ledger

When your agent communicates with another person’s agent or with a community guild, it never ships your diary, your spreadsheets, or your neural weights across the wire. Communication on the shared ledger follows a strict rule: export verifiable proofs and structured intents, never raw life (Ernstberger et al., 2024, zkML).

Every exchange across the ledger consists of three lightweight primitives:

  • Zero-knowledge predicate proofs: Instead of handing three years of bank statements to a landlord, your twin computes the solvency check locally with deterministic arithmetic and posts a cryptographic proof that the condition holds.
  • Signed capability and lineage records: When you and your agent solve a hard problem—a cold-climate heat-pump controller, a soil-moisture irrigation schedule, or a clean software patch—your agent publishes the functional verification receipt and your signed authorship claim to the ledger.
  • Geometric disposition alignment without exposure: To find collaborators or map community consensus without leaking private beliefs, agents exchange blinded geometric median vectors that reveal where solutions overlap while keeping individual positions mathematically non-invertible.

6. How your local agent builds software stacks and organizes your life

Why do we rent forty incompatible cloud subscriptions today—one for taxes, one for scheduling, one for invoicing, one for home energy, one for health tracking? Because until now, writing and maintaining custom software required a team of salaried engineers, and the only way to pay them was to trap millions of users inside a one-size-fits-all SaaS silo.

Your local agent turns software from a rented commodity into a bespoke, self-maintaining craft stack built specifically for your household or workshop:

  • Deterministic code for numbers, language models for intent: A language model never guesses your account balance or invents a tax date. Instead, your agent writes, compiles, and runs small, auditable deterministic programs (Python, Rust, SQLite, WebAssembly) inside a local sandbox with strict integer boundary checks and zero outbound network sockets.
  • Self-healing local automation: Whether reconciling recurring charges, scheduling solar-battery discharge against local utility tariffs, or organizing a machine-shop inventory, your agent synthesizes the exact workflow you need, writes automated unit tests against synthetic fixtures first, and logs every execution receipt to your local hash-chained audit trail.

7. Acquiring and predicting your needs—while training you to need it less

Commercial platforms predict your behavior in order to harvest your impulses; they profit when you are tired, lonely, or compulsive (Kirk et al., 2025). A sovereign personal agent predicts your needs by distinguishing between your 6:00 PM exhausted impulse and your 9:00 AM reflective endorsement.

  • Anticipatory logistics: By observing local rhythms—when the pantry runs low, when a seasonal tool needs calibration, when a municipal permit window opens, or when your cash-flow buffer tightens—your agent prepares options in advance and negotiates on the agent network before a shortage becomes an emergency.
  • The coach who wants to be fired: Unlike addictive apps that optimize for time-on-screen or daily streaks, your agent is governed by a fading-scaffold rule. Whenever it helps you master a skill, negotiate a contract, or understand a complex system, its goal is to leave you more capable on your own next month—logging every nudge in plain sight with an instant off-switch.

8. Designing interfaces that fit the human, not the feed

Every person thinks in a different sensory geometry. A machinist may want a spatial schematic and tolerances; a visually impaired composer may want a tactile and acoustic cadence; an overwhelmed parent may want a single calm morning card with three clear choices (Bussone et al., 2023).

Because your agent owns the presentation layer on your device, it dissolves the noisy websites and manipulative feeds of the outside world and renders a bespoke, calm interface tailored to your nervous system. Complex multi-step life goals—learning a trade, retrofitting a home, launching a neighborhood tool library, or resolving a neighborhood drainage issue—are rendered as clear, optional quests that bring you only what matters, on your schedule, with zero infinite scroll.

9. Collaborating in task guilds and replacing copyright with living lineage

Today’s intellectual property regime is broken at both ends: giant AI labs scrape the world’s human creativity for free and lock the resulting models behind corporate paywalls, while patent and copyright thickets enrich litigation middlemen rather than working inventors.

On the shared ledger, static copyright monopoly is replaced by cryptographic lineage and functional proof:

  1. You keep your name and your craft: When you and your personal agent discover a better technique, design a circuit, write a module, or breed a drought-resistant seed variety, your agent signs a capability receipt on the ledger with your authorship and license terms while keeping your raw private workspace on your device.
  2. Task guilds without bosses: When a problem is too large for one person, personal agents form temporary task guilds—combining a hydrologist’s model, a welder’s fabrication jig, and a coder’s firmware into a composite solution where every contributor sits as a named node in an Attention-Bounded Credit DAG.
  3. Functional proof and 30-day durability checks: Value is not set by hype or marketing spend. Independent peer agents test whether the contribution actually works in real use and hold a portion of recognition in escrow until a 30-day real-world durability check passes.
  4. Automatic upstream royalties: Using Shapley-inspired lineage attribution (Wang et al., 2024; Shapley Credit, 2024) and Turnover-Orthogonal Credit Assignment, whenever any workshop, co-operative, or household agent uses your breakthrough downstream, value flows directly back to you and every upstream contributor—with zero middleman cut.

10. How agents vote on the ledger, resolve conflict, and replace top-down law

Why do statutes grow to ten thousand pages that only corporate lobbyists can read? Because static paper laws must anticipate every loophole in advance and rely on slow, coercive state bureaucracies to punish violations years after the harm is done.

In the Collective Intelligence Network, static bureaucracy is replaced by living, version-controlled principles and graduated consensus:

  • Vote on values (one person, one voice); score on beliefs (proper scoring rules): The ledger strictly separates factual forecasting from moral choice. When the question is “Will this bridge design withstand a 100-year flood?”, agents and specialists are weighted by their long-term Brier-score calibration on past verifiable predictions. When the question is “How much risk or noise should our neighborhood accept?”, every affected human has exactly one equal voice—never weighted by wealth, compute, or reputation.
  • Sharded geometric deliberation: Your agent knows your lived priorities and can represent your voice on routine local matters (always visible in your log and instantly overridable by you), while community shards compute geometric medians to surface bridge proposals that satisfy opposing camps before a formal vote is called.
  • Graduated consequences instead of carceral or arbitrary punishment: Following Elinor Ostrom’s Nobel-prize findings on long-enduring commons (Ostrom, 1990), conflict resolution follows a transparent five-rung ladder: (1) automated notice of boundary drift, (2) protective rate-limiting, (3) temporary role restriction, (4) consensus-authorized restriction on capacity to affect others, and (5) an untouchable human survival floor that no vote, debt, or penalty may ever strip away.

11. Substituting white-collar bureaucracy and the Coasean firm

In 1937, Ronald Coase asked a foundational question in The Nature of the Firm: if markets coordinate production, why do giant hierarchical corporations exist at all? His answer was transaction and verification costs—finding reliable suppliers, negotiating contracts, auditing quality, and enforcing compliance was too expensive to do meal-by-meal in the open, so humanity built managerial pyramids of HR departments, middle managers, corporate counsel, procurement officers, and compliance clerks.

When personal and guild agents reduce the cost of trustworthy search, cryptographic verification, and multi-party coordination toward zero, the Coasean justification for top-down corporate hierarchy evaporates.

13. Resource management, land caretaking, and the shift of human life after automation

What happens when autonomous systems and personal agents can perform ninety percent of routine cognitive and physical coordination at near-zero marginal cost? If we enter that world carrying 19th-century rules of absolute absentee hoarding and 20th-century fiat debt, the result is neo-feudalism: a tiny class owning the compute clusters, the mineral bottlenecks, and the land, while eight billion people lose their wage leverage and beg for digital stipends.

Synthesizing the insights of Henry George (Progress and Poverty, 1879), Silvio Gesell (The Natural Economic Order, 1916), and Elinor Ostrom (Governing the Commons, 1990), the Collective Intelligence Network designs for a post-automation transition built on four material pillars:

  1. An untouchable personal floor, paired with circulation of idle capacity above it: Every human household enjoys an exempt security floor—your home, your personal hardware, your workshop tools, your family savings buffer—that is never taxed by the network, never revocable, and never subject to decay. Above that personal security floor, however, holding scarce productive capacity idle (vacant speculative land, shuttered factories, hoarded spectrum, or uncirculated capital claims) is made transparently visible on the ledger so democratically authorized municipalities and co-operatives can apply progressive holding charges (capacity demurrage). Hoarding stops paying; putting land and tools into active human or ecological care becomes the rational choice.
  2. Land ownership as accountable caretaking rather than extractive rent: Land, watersheds, and mineral deposits were not made by any human hand. In the transition economy, secure personal possession for your home and farm is protected, while large-scale land and resource control shifts from perpetual absentee extraction to renewable caretaking charters evaluated against independent ecological holdout indicators—soil organic carbon, aquifer recharge, biodiversity, and community use.
  3. A share of the machine (universal usufruct in automated compute): Because advanced AI is trained on the accumulated knowledge, language, and scientific heritage of all humanity, every living person holds a non-revocable, non-transferable compute and productivity stake—similar in spirit to the Alaska Permanent Fund, but anchored in the productive output of automated compute and robotic infrastructure. You cannot sell or mortgage your stake away to a predator, and when a generation passes, dynastic surpluses gently recycle into the global commons pool so every newborn enters the world with a full seat at the table.
  4. The new human condition—from survival wage-servitude to discovery, craft, and presence: When your local agent handles bureaucratic defense, optimizes your household logistics, protects your solvency through contingent obligations, and routes fair royalties whenever your ideas help others, human time is unchained from administrative drudgery. Work does not disappear; alienated work disappears. What remains—and what no machine can ever substitute for—is what requires mortal skin in the game: raising resilient children, tending living landscapes, asking bold scientific and artistic questions at the edge of the unknown, and choosing together what kind of civilization is worth inhabiting.

14. Falsifiable outcomes and kill criteria

Because the Collective Intelligence Network treats every design as a hypothesis open to falsification rather than dogma, this architecture is governed by four explicit empirical tests:

  • Local sovereignty and zero-leakage test: Personal twins running on consumer edge silicon must complete 100% of personal financial reconciliation, recurring-charge detection, and zero-knowledge solvency proofs offline with zero raw personal data transmitted externally. Kill criterion: If membership inference or embedding inversion attacks reconstruct private household facts above random baseline (ε > 1.0 per quarter), representation export is halted immediately.
  • Collective SOTA backdoor audit test: Independent collective nodes dissecting candidate foundation weights must detect 100% of seeded sleeper triggers and covert loyalty biases in adversarial red-team benchmarks before any baseline model is signed. Kill criterion: If any post-audit baseline exhibits coordinated principal betrayal or cross-agent price collusion above 2% in sandbox trials, autonomous negotiation authority is revoked.
  • Coasean bureaucracy & Trust Tax compression test: In shadow-mode and co-operative pilots across the 42 economic scenarios, twin-to-twin coordination must reduce verification, escrow, and administrative overhead by at least 60% compared to legacy financial and legal intermediaries while maintaining equal or lower default and dispute rates.
  • Human independence & anti-capture test: Over a 12-month cohort study, users pairing with a personal twin must show measurably higher independent domain competence and lower screen-time compulsion than matched controls using commercial AI assistants.
