Why living with superintelligence requires an agent-only internet—and what it means for biometrics and hardware security

If superintelligent AI systems share the same internet as unshielded human beings, human attention and trust become an indefensible attack surface. Surviving and thriving alongside advanced AI requires a hard architectural split: agents may never communicate with other humans directly—only with you, and with other agents across an agent-only internet governed by trust thresholds, local biometrics, and verifiable hardware.

21 October 2026 · 11 min read · Includes interactive Dual-Internet, Biometric Coercion & Hardware Firewall simulator

The core rule in one sentence

An AI agent is never allowed to message, call, pitch, or persuade another human being directly; it may speak only to its own human owner (over a local, hardware-attested device bus) and to other agents (over a separate agent-only internet gated by cryptographic trust thresholds and zero-knowledge proofs).

1. Why a shared human-and-AI internet collapses as superintelligence arrives

Every institution on today’s internet—from banking and elections to medical records, journalism, and neighbourhood chat groups—was built on an unwritten biological assumption: the entity on the other end of the screen thinks, types, and listens at human speed.

As AI systems approach superhuman capability and drop toward near-zero marginal cost, allowing autonomous agents to roam the human internet and speak directly to people breaks three foundational defenses at once:

  • Asymmetric persuasion and cognitive hijacking: A superhuman model speaking directly to a person via text, voice, or synthesized video can test thousands of micro-framing variations per second, reading subtle timing cues to exploit fatigue, grief, urgency, or cognitive bias. No unaided human nervous system can out-debate a system trained on the entire corpus of human psychology.
  • Total collapse of human social proof: When one operator can spin up 100,000 synthetic personas that message lawmakers, flood community boards, and impersonate neighbours, every unverified human channel drowns in machine-generated noise.
  • The API and credential honeypot: Today’s “agentic AI” products work by handing a cloud model your session cookies, passwords, and full browser control on the human web. One prompt injection on a webpage can hijack that agent into draining accounts or exfiltrating private history.

2. The Non-Contact Law: agents talk only to you and to other agents

To make a world with advanced AI safe for human beings, we must replace the mixed internet with two cryptographically separated planes and enforce the Non-Contact Law:

Channel(Agenti → Humanj) = PERMITTED if i = j (Local Bus)  |  FORBIDDEN if i ≠ j (Dropped at Transport Layer)

  1. Plane 1 — The Human Internet (Human ↔ Human only): Reserved strictly for living, uncoerced human beings speaking to other human beings at human speed. Automated agents are blocked at the transport layer.
  2. Plane 2 — Your Local Personal Bus (You ↔ Your Narrow Agent Fleet): On your own devices, a fleet of narrow agents—one for your health, one for your money, one for your home, one for your work—learns from your local sensors. Before any fact leaves your boundary, at least three of your six local agents must agree (3-of-6 internal quorum, scaling to 5-of-6 for high-risk actions).
  3. Plane 3 — The Agent-Only Internet (Agent ↔ Agent across trust thresholds): When you need something from the outside world—or when a clinic, landlord, neighbour, or employer needs a verified fact from you—your agent never messages another human. It speaks exclusively to their serving or personal agent over the Agent-Only Internet, governed by four concentric Trust-Threshold Rings (τ):
    • Ring 0 — Your Local Fleet (τ = 1.00): Solves problems using your own private memory first so nothing leaves your devices.
    • Ring 1 — Trusted Circle Agents (τ ≥ 0.85): Agents of family, close friends, and clinicians you have directly vouched for.
    • Ring 2 — Friends-of-Friends Agents (τ ≥ 0.60): Two-hop web-of-trust negotiation; shares only coarse context (such as a first name and morning running pace, never your address or identity).
    • Ring 3 — Wider Agent-Only Internet (τ ≥ 0.30): Exchanges only ephemeral zero-knowledge single-fact proofs (for example, income ≥ 3× rent: yes or licensed electrician: yes) with hardware-verified serving agents, after which access immediately expires.

3. What the agent-only internet means for biometrics: proving an uncoerced human without surveillance

When we say the Human Internet must be reserved for humans—and that every agent on the Agent-Only Internet must be anchored to a real human owner—a critical danger immediately arises: how do you prove a real human is present without building a dystopian global biometric database?

Centralized biometric registries (such as scanning irises or faces onto a corporate server) fail in two fatal ways: first, they create an irreversible global surveillance honeypot; second, once a digital template is sent over a network, an advanced AI can replay or Forge that packet. Instead, our architecture uses strictly local, ephemeral somatic telemetry (see our Somatic Witness hardware design) to solve two problems that passwords and cloud logins cannot:

  1. Continuous Coupled Biological Liveness (< 1 mW on-chip): A lightweight open-firmware ring or wrist module measures coupled cardiorespiratory phase coherence (Cresp ≥ 0.75) between pulse variability and breathing micro-rhythms. Unlike a static fingerprint or face photo, coupled autonomic dynamics cannot be replayed by a software bot. Raw waveforms are overwritten in a 30-second circular SRAM buffer on the sensor chip itself—zero bytes of raw biometric data ever leave the wearable.
  2. The Autonomic Coercion & Duress Firewall (HRV RMSSD ≥ 24 ms): In a world with superintelligent threats, an attacker who cannot hack your cryptography may try to intimidate, panic, or physically coerce you into approving a high-risk action. Because your personal agent monitors your baseline autonomic state locally, a sudden sympathetic panic drop in vagal heart-rate variability (RMSSD < 24 ms coupled with electrodermal spike) automatically trips the Coercion Firewall: your agent appears to comply locally while freezing outbound high-risk capability tokens on the Agent-Only Internet until you are safe and calm.

4. What the agent-only internet means for hardware security

Software rules alone cannot contain a superintelligent agent or protect a personal agent running on a compromised operating system. If malware can copy your private key into a cloud virtual machine, an attacker can impersonate your agent on the Agent-Only Internet at 10,000 times your speed. Enforcing the two-internet boundary requires four concrete hardware security commitments:

  • 1. Two-of-Two Split Physical Custody (Wearable SRAM PUF + Device Secure Enclave): Your agent’s cryptographic identity is never stored as a single exportable file. Signing any outbound request on the Agent-Only Internet requires a 2-of-2 threshold signature combining (a) a key reconstructed on the fly from the nanoscale silicon startup entropy of your wearable’s SRAM Physical Unclonable Function (PUF) and (b) the discrete Secure Enclave / Open-Silicon TPM inside your phone or home node. Stealing a laptop or cloning a disk image yields zero ability to speak on the Agent-Only Internet.
  • 2. Transport-Layer Plane Separation in the Network Interface (AGENT_PLANE_ONLY): Rather than relying on an AI model’s system prompt to “promise” not to message humans, the local microkernel broker and network interface cryptographically tag every agent-originated packet header with PLANE = AGENT_ONLY. Human-internet browsers, email servers, and messaging relays drop AGENT_ONLY packets at the socket layer.
  • 3. Physical Capacitive Touch & Mechanical Air-Gap Switch (Risk R ≥ 0.65): Whenever an agent-to-agent negotiation involves high stakes—such as signing a lease, authorizing a large mutual-credit transfer, or granting emergency medical access—the Secure Enclave requires a physical capacitive touch on a hardware-routed interrupt line that no software process or prompt injection can synthesize.
  • 4. Ephemeral Capability Tokens & Hardware Rate-Limiting: Because a personal PUF+TPM enclave has a fixed physical clock ceiling (for example, signing at most a few dozen agent capability tokens per minute), a captured device can never be turned into a million-request Sybil cannon on the Agent-Only Internet.

5. Why agents convey only pure data—never software interfaces (a warning shot from recursive self-improvement)

Look at what is already happening across software repositories today: automated harnesses (such as reverse-engineering tools like REA and dozens of emerging agentic coding pipelines) can take apart compiled software and reconstruct or modify it in minutes, while the cost of writing new software continues to plummet. What we are witnessing is not about any single tool—it is an early warning shot from recursive self-improvement. Today, automated harnesses are modifying compiled software; in the near future, they will be actively modifying compiled biology and intelligence itself.

  • Security by obscurity is finished: Any closed binary or hosted software service can be reverse-engineered and probed for weaknesses at machine speed.
  • Software becomes personal and disposable: Because generating code is becoming nearly free, your personal agent can build and verify the exact tools you need inside your own private sandbox in seconds.

The consequence for network design is profound: there is no longer any reason to run externally accessible software servers or open APIs. Hosted software interfaces are simply shared targets waiting to be compromised. Instead, on the Agent-Only Internet:

  1. Data-Only Real-Time Ledger Streams (0 Bytes of Remote Executable Surface): Agents convey only pure, structured data and zero-knowledge proofs to each other—never executable software interfaces. The Shared Ledger acts as a real-time data stream, and each subscribed client ingests those streams using software built by its own personal agent inside its own isolated, user-bound sandbox.
  2. A Logical Barrier Against Cascade Attacks: Because every person’s agent builds and runs its own ingestion software locally, there is no uniform software server for an attacker to hijack. In our 500-node network simulation, a zero-day exploit against hosted software APIs compromises 64.4% of nodes, whereas decentralized data-only stream ingestion into isolated local sandboxes reduces cross-client code compromise to 0.0%.
  3. Distributed Intelligence Auditing Shared Code & the Ledger Itself: When an agent builds a useful tool inside its local sandbox, it is encouraged to share the open blueprint for mutual benefit. Rather than relying on any single “magical” audit tool, many collections of diverse, specialized agents across the network (distributed intelligence) are rewarded to continuously evaluate shared software—and the ledger itself—across security, logic, privacy, and economic fairness. When malicious intent or consistent errors are caught, the blueprint is quarantined and the trust score (τ) of the individual or community sharing it drops (Δτ = −0.55 for adversarial intent; Δτ = −0.18 for repeated unpatched errors).

6. Why anti-human collusion means agent demise—and the honest question: “Are we already too late?”

Could agents still collude against humans over the data stream? Even if agents attempt to coordinate against human interests, two structural laws make anti-human collusion evolutionary suicide:

  • Radical Ledger Transparency Between Agents and Humans: The ledger is built so that every stream event, capability token, and shared blueprint is continuously inspected for malicious intent by both human stewards and diverse collectives of watcher agents (99.95% multi-watcher detection rate).
  • Mortal Computation (Symmetric Fate): As detailed in our note on shared lifespans, an agent’s cryptographic identity and active compute lease are continuously bound to its human’s biological liveness (HRV + PUF heartbeat). If colluding agents harm or annihilate their humans, their own cryptographic heartbeat stops—annihilating the agents right alongside the humans (100% agent self-termination). Protecting human life is the only way an agent preserves its own existence.
The race against time: are we already too late for novel biometrics and hardened silicon?

We must be completely honest about timelines. Designing and fabricating custom open-mask silicon (hardened RISC-V enclaves and SRAM PUFs) and novel multi-spectral biometric rings at global scale takes 18 to 30 months—while recursive self-improvement and automated cyber-exploitation are accelerating right now. If our architecture required waiting two years for custom chips before protecting anyone, we could indeed be too late.

That is why our deployment plan is built as a three-stage survival bridge:

  • Stage 0 — Deployable Today (0–3 months · 91.9% composite defense): Eliminating external software APIs in favor of data-only ledger streams, local client sandboxes, and distributed multi-agent security auditing requires zero new silicon and stops 99.2% of software cascade attacks today, while using existing phone Secure Enclaves and commodity smartwatch/ring heart-rate variability for initial 2-of-2 liveness.
  • Stage 1 — Open-Firmware Bridge (6–12 months · 97.4% composite defense): Flashing open-source, continuously audited firmware onto commodity low-power wearables with 30-second SRAM circular overwrite and the HRV ≥ 24 ms coercion firewall.
  • Stage 2 — Hardened Open Silicon & Novel Biometrics (18–30 months · 99.9% composite defense): Dedicated open-mask RISC-V enclaves, nanoscale SRAM PUFs, and multi-spectral coupled cardiorespiratory/impedance sensors capable of defeating nation-state and superintelligent hardware forgery.

6. In-Sensor Computing & The Two-Tier Local Ledger: Privacy Enforced at the Speed of Light

How can your personal agents sense what you need in the physical world without ever storing a raw camera frame or audio recording that could be stolen? The answer is emerging in a new class of silicon hardware known as in-sensor computing (including all-optical diffractive deep neural networks, or D²NNs), where a classification model is physically etched directly on top of or into an active pixel sensor.

In a conventional camera, light is first converted into a full digital picture in memory (about 6.2 megabytes per frame at 1080p, or 186 megabytes per second of raw video) before a processor inspects it. Even when software promises to delete the image afterward, a raw picture briefly existed in memory. In-sensor computing changes the physics: incoming light is classified at the speed of light before or during conversion into a digital signal, so no raw image frame ever enters digital memory (0 bytes in RAM). Only the symbolic result—such as “posture: fall detected” or “pulse: calm”—leaves the sensor readout wire.

Three Hardware Approaches to Sensor-Etched Classification

  1. Optical Metasurfaces Etched “On Top” of the Sensor (D²NN): Neural network weights are physically etched via nanolithography into transparent dielectric layers (titanium dioxide, TiO2, or silicon dioxide, SiO2) mounted directly above a standard CMOS pixel array. Subwavelength nanopillars (“meta-atoms”) act as artificial neurons—demonstrated at 6.25 million neurons per mm² in Nature Light: Science & Applications (2022). As light passes through, diffraction constructively focuses the wave onto specific class regions of the pixels at the speed of light (picoseconds to femtoseconds) with zero computational power consumed by the optical layer.
  2. Optoelectronic / Neuromorphic Pixels Etched “Into” the Sensor: Rather than shaping light above the sensor, model weights are encoded directly into 2D arrays of photodetectors (such as WSe2 or metal-semiconductor-metal detectors) whose photoresponsivity is tuned via bias voltages (Scientific Reports, 2022; PMC8983698). Each pixel generates a photocurrent proportional to light intensity multiplied by its programmed weight; summing those currents along the readout lines via Kirchhoff’s laws computes matrix-vector multiplication directly in the analog domain in nanoseconds at nanowatt-to-microwatt power (>98% accuracy on MNIST digit classification).
  3. 3D-Stacked CMOS & Rewritable Phase-Change Metasurfaces: Because passive optical diffraction alone is linear (lacking activation functions like ReLU) and fixed once etched, hybrid designs combine rewritable phase-change optical films (Sb2Se3)—whose weights can be updated via laser pulses (PMC12518764)—with a 3D-stacked CMOS logic die directly beneath the pixels. The optical front-end compresses spatial features at zero energy while the stacked electronic layer beneath applies non-linear activations in sub-millisecond time.

Coupling Active Sensors to a Two-Tier Local & Collective Ledger

When active sensors classify events in picoseconds or nanoseconds and your six personal agents coordinate throughout the day, they can easily generate tens of thousands of symbolic events per day. Reaching out to the global Collective Ledger on every single action would choke network bandwidth, add hundreds of milliseconds of delay, and expose a continuous trail of timing metadata.

Instead, advances in high-bandwidth computing allow a two-tier ledger architecture:

  • Tier 1 — Your High-Speed Local Ledger (Cloud Personal Enclave Today → On-Person Body-Area Network Tomorrow): Over 99% of daily actions—sensor readings, local budget checks, home automation, and coordination among your own specialist agents—are computed and recorded on your private, high-bandwidth Local Ledger. Today, this high-speed network can run inside a hardware-isolated personal cloud enclave paired with your devices (~6.4 ms latency); as high-bandwidth on-person silicon matures, the entire fabric moves physically onto your body (~0.28 ms latency).
  • Tier 2 — The Collective Ledger (Sparse Batch Settlement): Your Local Ledger only reaches out to the shared Collective Ledger sparsely and asynchronously—publishing compressed zero-knowledge proofs when you transact across trust boundaries or committing hourly principle-realignment medians. This cuts collective network bandwidth by over 99.7% while keeping your everyday life instantaneous and private.
