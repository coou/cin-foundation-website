Spotlight signal (October 2026): “We were looking at it wrong” — Nick Cammarata & Goodfire on why Neural Networks Are Already Clean

On 19 October 2026, MIT Technology Review highlighted a major upheaval in AI interpretability sparked by Nick Cammarata (a pioneer of mechanistic interpretability, formerly of OpenAI’s Clarity team and now at the interpretability lab Goodfire) in his 26,000-word research essay, “Neural Networks Are Already Clean.”

What is the claim—and why did it upend the AI safety debate?

  • The old consensus (polysemantic chaos & superposition): For the past five years, most interpretability researchers assumed that neural networks cram far more concepts than they have neurons into a tangled, chaotic mess (“superposition”), where a single neuron appears to fire for unrelated things like cat faces and car grilles. Under that view, auditing a model required training massive secondary models (Sparse Autoencoders) in corporate datacenters just to untangle what a network is thinking.
  • Cammarata’s reversal (“Already Clean”): Re-examining the very consensus he helped establish in 2020, Cammarata argues that in modern, sufficiently capable models, the vast majority of native neurons and activation subspaces already have a single, legible, structured job. Famous examples of “tangled” neurons were largely artifacts of tiny early architectures, weak visualization tools, or human mislabeling (for instance, the “cat face + car grille” neuron is actually a clean geometric V-contour detector).
  • Scientific caution & how we test it: Veterans such as Neel Nanda (Google DeepMind) and researchers in the Chris Olah (Anthropic) circuits tradition have rightly noted that this working hypothesis still requires rigorous peer-reviewed replication, particularly in middle transformer layers and under adversarial pressure where covert superposition or steganographic channels could still hide.

Three ways this directly accelerates the Collective Intelligence Network

  1. Lightweight, on-device taste distillation (Reclaiming your profile): If native neural activations are already structured along clean concept axes, your personal agent (1B–8B local model) does not need a billion-dollar cloud Sparse Autoencoder to separate what genuinely fulfills you over 30 days (ztelic) from late-night outrage traps (zlimbic). It can read and filter those native concept directions directly on your phone or laptop in milliseconds.
  2. Direct native-circuit auditing at the runtime gate (No one commands a swarm): When open-weight models from US, European, or Chinese labs are submitted to run as personal twins, independent watchers can audit their native weight subspaces directly for sleeper backdoors or hidden corporate biases—pairing Cammarata’s native-unit inspection with Neel Nanda’s and Buck Shlegeris’s adversarial control tests.
  3. Symbolic-vector git merge on our Principle Realignment Test-Net: Because native activation axes align cleanly with human-legible concepts, each participant’s agent can project lived friction directly onto our 12-dimensional symbolic principle basis (vu,k ∈ ℝ12) and cohere community updates via our Sharded Weiszfeld Geometric Median (Subspace-Preserving Geometric Median & TIES-Merging Weight Aggregation (SH-WGM-Upsert)).

Why we use “least-resistance” open scholar statistics

Rather than building a brittle internal citation scraper or inventing proprietary impact scores for outside scientists, our research pipeline follows the principle of least resistance: every researcher and lab below is connected directly to live, open bibliometric indices—Google Scholar (citation counts, h-index, i10-index), Semantic Scholar (influential citations & citation velocity), OpenAlex (open knowledge graph), and arXiv (verified preprints). Both human readers and personal agents can query those live indices in one click.
