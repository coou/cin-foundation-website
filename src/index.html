Our Vision

Beyond Traditional Systems

The Collective Intelligence Network aims to give people better information and reasoning about their own lives, their communities and their environment.

Many institutions today are slow, siloed, opaque or costly to deal with. We want to help people understand and navigate them, and to work alongside them, not replace them.

We propose to do this with tools that people own and control, checked by independent observers, and tested in stages before anyone relies on them.

The Four Pillars of Our Vision

More Time, Long Term

If AI agents take over routine institutional coordination, people could spend less time on administration. A long-term hope, not a promise.

Many people spend long hours on paperwork, coordination and administration that software could in principle handle.

If many of these tasks, especially the "soft labor" of coordinating and managing information, were handled by AI agents under human control, people would have more time. We treat this as a long-term hypothesis to test, not a promise.

The CIN holds no money and runs no economy. Its role is to supply information and reasoning, and to make the evidence behind claims visible.

Contribution Without Disclosure

Contribution can be recognized, but sharing personal information is never rewarded and declining to share costs nothing.

People should be able to get help, and have their contribution recognized, without handing over broader access to their data than the specific fact being checked.

Services built on the CIN may ask for answers, never raw data. Contribution may be recognized; disclosure is never paid and never penalized.

Any measure of trust about a person is held by that person and shared only if they choose, as evidence of reliability. It is never a currency and never a public ranking of people.

Human-AI Symbiosis

A balanced ecosystem where humans and AI collaborate as partners, with personalized cognitive assistants enhancing daily life and well-being.

Human-AI symbiosis describes a future where humans and AI agents collaborate effectively towards shared purposes, aiming for a balanced ecosystem rather than AI domination. This deep integration acknowledges AI agents not merely as tools, but as collaborators that can reason, learn, and work alongside humans.

A central component facilitating this symbiosis is the personalized agentic layer, also conceptualized as a "digital twin" or "cognitive partner" for each participant. This digital twin is an intelligent, adaptive interface and assistant, designed primarily to enhance the user's well-being.

In daily life, this agentic twin would function as a sophisticated translator and mediator between users and the network. It could formulate requests based on a user's intent, help manage a portfolio of skills and contributions, enhance physical comfort by automating routine tasks, foster emotional comfort by aligning a user's path with their interests, and lead to temporal abundance by freeing up human time for personal growth and creativity.

Visible Power, Private People

Scrutiny grows with influence: those with more power are more visible. People's own information stays with them.

Decisions that affect many people, and the evidence behind them, should be open to scrutiny, and the more influence someone has, the more visible their actions should be. Individuals are different: people own their information and share only what they choose.

Trust is earned through verification by independent observers. A signature shows where a record came from, not that it is true. We work with regulated institutions rather than around them.

Anyone may leave with their data, and communities may decline, adapt or fork what we build. No single person, organization or agent should become indispensable.

Entropy and Order: A Kitchen Analogy

An analogy for reducing disorder in physical and factual domains

Imagine your kitchen is a chaotic mess after a big cooking session – dirty dishes piled everywhere, spices spilled, food scraps on the counter. This messy state is like a system with high entropy, representing a lot of disorder and uncertainty.

Now, imagine you spend an hour cleaning that kitchen. You wash the dishes, wipe down the counters, put everything back in its place. When you're done, the kitchen is sparkling clean and organized. That act of cleaning reduced the entropy of your kitchen.

Cleaning transforms chaos into order and clarity.

In the Collective Intelligence Network (CIN), reducing disorder is a goal for physical systems, ecosystems and factual questions: for example, clarifying a complex problem or building stable knowledge. It is never a goal for people's beliefs, cultures or values, which the CIN protects. The CIN does not use entropy reduction to price work or to measure a person's worth.

Just as a clean kitchen makes future cooking easier and more predictable, clearer facts make shared decisions easier.

Signed vs Unsigned Observations

How the CIN values information based on its verifiable origin

In the Collective Intelligence Network (CIN), the origin and integrity of information are crucial for establishing trust. This is where the distinction between "signed" and "unsigned" observations becomes vital.

A signed observation shows where a record came from and that it has not changed since. It does not show that the record is accurate. An unsigned observation from the web is like an anonymous tip and needs checking. Important claims need agreement among independent observers.

Signed Observation from a Trusted Sensor:

Imagine a sensor (like a thermometer or camera) equipped with secure device attestation or cryptographic capabilities. When this sensor records data, it creates a digital signature that cryptographically links the data to the sensor's verified identity and proves that the data hasn't been tampered with since it was recorded.

Unsigned Observation from the Web:

This refers to any data or information found on the broader internet that lacks verifiable cryptographic proof of its origin or integrity. While such information might be useful, the CIN cannot inherently verify its authenticity or accuracy.

Without a verifiable "signature" of origin and integrity, unsigned observations are treated with significant skepticism until they are checked by independent observers.

Bounded Compute for Artificial Agents

Why artificial agents get a limit on the compute they can use

The CIN proposes that the compute available to artificial agents be bounded by a contract scaled to their intelligence and their ability to optimize. The bound can shrink over time, or grow as people adopt interfaces that keep them coupled to that intelligence.

Artificial agents serve and promote biological life. No vote or action of an artificial agent may be for that agent's own benefit without approval by people. The CIN applies this contract to its own agents.

This is a design proposal under research, not a deployed system. Its purpose is to keep artificial agents bounded and answerable to people.

Ethical Sandboxing & Social Experimentation

How the CIN enables safe testing of new governance models

The Collective Intelligence Network's inherently modular design, combined with its emphasis on simulation and adaptive governance, aims to support "ethical sandboxing" and careful social experimentation.

Rather than rigid, top-down mandates, the CIN allows for the safe prototyping and testing of novel governance rules, economic models, and ethical protocols within controlled, agent-based simulations before widespread implementation. Simulations may recommend or discard options; they never authorize.

This means communities can collaboratively design and iteratively refine their shared "CIN contracts", observe their emergent outcomes, and dynamically adapt to challenges, aiming for an evolution of collective intelligence that is resilient and aligned with human well-being.

Staged trials reduce the risk of broad, untested change by allowing controlled experimentation and gradual, evidence-based adoption, starting with Shadow Mode, where a proposal is measured but has no effect.
