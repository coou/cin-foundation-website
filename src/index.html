The idea

Information, not custody.

Most digital services become useful by collecting you. We are designing the opposite: tools that become useful while everything about you stays on your own devices.

01

Your data lives with you

Your twin runs on your own computer. You can see, export and delete everything it knows, and it sends nothing anywhere without your explicit action.

02

Answers, not access

When a service needs to know something, it gets proof of that one fact, not your history. Declining to share costs you nothing, and sharing is never paid.

Useful alone, first

Every tool must help one person before it depends on many. The CIN continues only if people find it useful, and it is funded by voluntary donations, never by engagement or data.

What we're building

Three instruments, specified before built.

Each one is written down as a specification with stated design rules, test thresholds and kill criteria before any code runs on anyone's data. None of them is available yet.

The personal twin

A private assistant that lives on your computer and works for you alone.

  • Finds forgotten subscriptions, price increases and unfair fees in your own bank exports.
  • Tracks renewals and deadlines from bills and documents you add.
  • Remembers your decisions and how they turned out.
  • Money figures and matches come from deterministic code, never from a language model.
SpecifiedSpec 01 · 04

The agent runtime

The cage every agent runs in, including ours.

  • Each agent has a written contract: what it may read, call and spend, and when its mandate expires.
  • All access is denied by default and granted through a broker.
  • Tamper-evident logs record every action; anything that leaves the device is ledgered.
  • Any agent can be suspended at once.

The Observatory

An honest record of where the world is heading relative to our goals.

  • Indicators for personal AI, networks, money, interfaces and democratic participation.
  • Signs of moving away from our goals get equal space.
  • Goals become scored forecasts, so misses stay on the record.
  • Quarterly reports with a corrections section.

Why now

Small agents, loyal to one person.

AI agents now act through the same computers, phones and services that people use. They compete with us for the same levers, at a speed and scale no person can watch.

We think part of the answer is to fracture intelligence instead of concentrating it: alongside large general models, small bounded agents that each serve one person and hold the keys to that person's information and systems. When a powerful model needs something, it asks the person's agent, which checks the request, answers narrowly, and logs what happened.

The answer to a threatening scenario is fewer privileges, independent observers and bounded agents, never more authority.

This works alongside regulation, lab commitments and independent safety research. It doesn't replace them. Concept under study

Work in the open

Built by AI agents, held to written rules.

Most of the CIN's documents, and this website, are drafted by AI agents working under a published operating loop and reviewed by a human steward. An agent doing helpful work should be easy to inspect, so here is how it works.

  1. ReadLoad the rules, the governing documents and the task list.
  2. PickChoose the next task by priority: safety and privacy first.
  3. BranchOne task, one branch. Nothing touches the main line.
  4. Do & verifyMake the change, test it, mark anything unverified.
  5. Sign & reportSigned commit with provenance; a written report.
  6. ReviewA person approves before anything is merged or published.

What our agents may never do

  • Weaken or remove any of the 19 floor rights.
  • Add telemetry, analytics or any undeclared network call.
  • Send a person's private twin data to a remote model.
  • Produce financial numbers, dates or matches with a language model.
  • Present an unverified citation as fact.
  • Publish, deploy or contact anyone without human approval.

The constitutional floor

Nineteen rights no majority can remove.

No protocol decision, operating agent, partner organization or majority can remove these. Each heading opens the full text from the CIN Constitution, version 0.6.

F1Ownership+

Every person can see, export in open formats, and delete all data the CIN holds about them, on their own devices.

F2Answers, not data+

No service, partner or mission organization may require broader access to a person's data than a proof of the specific fact it needs.

F3Disclosure is never paid and never penalized+

Contribution may be recognized; disclosure may not be rewarded, and declining to disclose costs nothing.

F4Due process for consequences+

Consequences follow specific, evidenced conduct, with notice, the opportunity to respond, proportionality, appeal and a path to restoration. Protective measures against active threats may take effect immediately but expire unless ratified.

F5Uncertainty falls on the system+

When the network is uncertain about a person, the burden of that uncertainty falls on the network, not the person.

F6No ranking of persons+

Representations of people's dispositions, histories or trustworthiness are never combined into a ranking of human worth, and never grant influence for proximity to the majority.

F7Equal voice on values; influence on facts follows record+

On questions of value, each affected person has one voice, and no wealth, hardware or assessed contribution changes its weight. On questions of fact, influence follows a scored forecasting record. Amending this floor requires broad participation, so no concentrated group can amend it alone.

F8Simulated people don't vote+

Simulated populations may inform deliberation, labeled as such, but never count as consent. A person's own twin voting on their behalf is representation of a real person, not simulation.

F9Standing is granted by people+

Agents, robots and collectives have standing only to the extent people configure, and that standing may be revoked.

F10Exit and fork+

Any person may leave with their data, and any community may fork the protocol.

F11Community authority+

Communities hosting the CIN control local data and local parameters above this floor, and may decline or withdraw the CIN.

F12Claims without conditions+

Recognition or rewards owed to contributors never depend on their joining or agreeing with the CIN.

F13No engagement optimization+

No CIN system measures or optimizes time spent, sessions, streaks or similar engagement metrics.

F14Protected diversity+

Reducing disorder is a goal for infrastructure, ecosystems and factual questions, never for human beliefs, cultures or values.

F15Right to rest+

Anyone may disconnect from all CIN systems at any time without loss of standing, records or any support that depends on CIN information.

F16Protection of autotelic activity+

Play, art, grief, contemplation and rest are never classified as waste or subjected to optimization.

F17Inner sovereignty+

No overlay, narrative or data stream may be placed in a person's interface without their explicit, revocable consent.

F18Existence does not require usefulness+

No person, community or ecosystem must justify its existence by contribution.

F19No force+

The CIN never deploys, directs or authorizes physical force.

Some texts are lightly shortened for the web (F4, F7, F9); the Constitution's wording governs. Until a ratified constitutional process exists, a founding steward maintains the document and may not weaken any of these rights.
