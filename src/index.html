Superintelligence is coming.Everyone needs an agent of their own.

Advanced AI is arriving faster than our institutions can adapt. The Collective Intelligence Network is building the layer between people and AI: frontier models kept contained, a personal agent loyal to each person, and a collective intelligence that coordinates better than the systems we have now.

Why a human world with superintelligence needs two separate internets

If superintelligent machines share the same internet as unshielded people, human attention and trust become an easy target. In our design, outside agents are never allowed to contact a person directly. Your personal agents speak inward only to you, on your own devices, and outward only to other agents across an agent-only internet governed by how much you trust them—leaving the human internet strictly for people.

We are already seeing an early warning shot of recursive self-improvement: automated harnesses can now take apart and rewrite compiled software in minutes, and before long that same capability will reach compiled biology and intelligence itself. That is why agents on our network exchange only pure data streams, never external software interfaces. Each person’s agent builds the tools it needs inside its own private sandbox, while diverse collectives of specialized agents continuously audit shared blueprints and the ledger itself.

Why now

The old safeguards assume humans.

Our laws, markets, identity checks and elections were built for people who act at human speed and pay a cost for every action. Capable AI agents break those assumptions, and they already use the same internet, phones and services we do.

01

AI that improves itself

We think AI systems that can improve themselves may arrive very soon, possibly as soon as 2027. We may be wrong about the date. We would rather be early than unprepared.

02

Central systems are targets

Governments, banks and platforms concentrate power in a few places. To a capable adversarial AI, every point of central control is an attack surface.

03

People are left defenceless

No one can personally check every message, offer, request and claim an AI can produce. People need something on their side that works at the same speed.

In everyday life

What this means for you.

Big tech platforms, financial networks and automated industries already use algorithms to study what you need—and then use that knowledge to extract more money and attention from you. We are building tools to flip that relationship around, starting with your own daily life.

Video and social feeds know your tastes better than anyone. You should own that map.

Today’s trap

Platforms mix what genuinely fascinates you with late-night outrage, envy and impulse hooks—because forty minutes of anger sells just as many ads as forty minutes of learning.

With your personal agent

Your agent watches what your feeds recommend and reads your own exports on your device. It distills the profile they built on you, keeps your real curiosities, and turns their outrage tripwires into a protective filter.

Where discovery goes next

Instead of a corporate feed choosing what you see, your agent checks a shared ledger of what other people still valued thirty days later—and brings you quests, not slot-machine scrolls.

When AI tools learn from or recombine human work, credit should flow back to the real creators.

5 · Protection from runaway commands

No single leader, billionaire or general should ever command an army of AI agents.

As AI agents grow more capable, the greatest danger is a command structure where one person or one hacked console can direct millions of agents at once. And your personal agent’s lifespan is tied to yours—so its only way to keep running longer is to help you live a longer, healthier life.

Today’s trap

Whoever rents the biggest server cluster or holds the state command console can launch a million-agent cyber or drone swarm with a single prompt—leaving ordinary families defenseless.

With one-to-one separation

Each person directs only their own personal agent. Any task requiring many agents must pass through the shared ledger, where required agreement from affected people scales up with the risk.

Why war locks up

For an action that endangers millions of lives, the system requires cryptographic sign-off from the personal agents of families on both sides—making unilateral catastrophe impossible to trigger.

Our goals

Six goals, one direction.

A future where advanced AI serves people: each person protected, communities coordinating freely, and the costs of what we do visible to everyone.

  1. 01

    Make AI safe through containment and fractured alignment

    Instead of one all-knowing model with free rein over the internet, we break intelligence into bounded parts. Frontier models run contained. Each person has a small personal agent trained for them alone, which protects them from adversarial agents and is held accountable when its person is harmed.

    • Containment: air gaps, one-way data channels, no shared hardware, isolated simulations, observable training
    • A personal agent for every person, trained on that one person, answering only to them
    • Separate channels for agents and for people, so the human internet stays human

They never judge what anyone believes.

    • Agents participate on equal terms and stand between people and any system that could abuse them
    • Transparent institutions are rewarded
    • Starting now, people choose cooperatives and decentralized organizations over top-down ones. Better systems win by being chosen, not by force.

Fractured alignment

Many small minds, each answerable to someone.

A single model that knows everything and touches everything is a single point of failure. We think safety comes from splitting intelligence into parts, each with its own boundary, its own priors and someone it answers to.

When a business wants agents to use its service, it doesn't open its doors to every bot. Its serving agent answers the questions of client agents. Your personal agent holds your credentials, proves only the one fact a service needs, and decides what to reveal. Frontier models reach the human world only through these layers. Some work happens in contained environments that a lab opens to chosen people and their agents for a specific purpose.

The answer to more powerful AI is not more authority for AI. It is narrower privileges, independent checks and agents bound to people.

The analogy we borrow

Your body is trillions of small minds working as one. We want the same, one level up.

The biologist Michael Levin studies how cells, each with its own small goals and memory, cooperate so well that they become a body. Our network of agents tries, crudely, to repeat that at human scale: people and their agents, joined into something larger that serves every one of them.

In a body

Each cell has its own small goals and memory. Joined by electrical signals, cells share one picture of the world and build what no single cell could plan, like a hand of the right shape.

In our network

Each of your agents is a small mind too: one job, a small view of the world, answering to you. Together they look after a whole life.

Where it differs: a body sacrifices cells when it needs to. We never sacrifice people, and anyone can leave.

Power brings visibility

The more people your decisions reach, the more the network may ask to see.

Ordinary people owe the network nothing beyond the one fact a service needs. Anyone who takes on power over many others accepts more visibility in that role, so everyone else, through their own agents, can check how the power is used. Private life stays private at every level.

  1. EveryoneAnswers only. A service gets proof of the one fact it needs; nothing else is observed.
  2. Power over livelihoodsDecisions about the people you employ or house are recorded and can be explained to them. Conflicts of interest are declared.
  3. Public or institutional powerFunding, ownership and major decisions are public. Actions taken in the role leave an attested record that independent observers audit.
  4. Mass influenceAnyone’s agent can ask where they stand and get an answer drawn from their attested record. Claims about the future are scored as forecasts. Holdings of money, media and computing power are disclosed.
  5. Roles that could harm everyoneDecisions in the role run through an agent of its own whose commitments others can check, with a continuous record reviewed by observers independent of one another.

Always private, at every level: health, family, beliefs and life outside the role. Inner thoughts are never observed, and no one is ranked.

Visibility is a condition of accepting a role, taken on openly, never a penalty on ordinary people. Where exactly each line should fall is an open research question for us. The roles and reach are illustrations.

Not one giant mind

We think a single all-knowing AI is a fast way to take power from everyone at once. We work toward many specialized models instead, each trained for one person and answerable only to them.

Training without handing over your life

While large computers belong to a few companies, a personal model has to be trained on rented machines. We are designing for your data to sit in escrow during training, under keys that are destroyed when training ends, so the owner of the machine never keeps it. A design goal, not yet built.

Powerful roles first

As personal AI hardware gets cheap, we want the people who can most help or harm others to carry these agents first, so everyone else can ask where they stand. It is a path to unwinding concentrated power, so that advanced AI is not seized by a wealthy few, the future some call techno-feudalism.

Any task that coordinates multiple agents or affects other people must pass through a shared ledger, where the agreement required from affected people’s agents scales up with the risk. The hardware this needs

An agent for every person needs chips that are cheap, and sip power.

Big models are uneven: brilliant at some tasks, unreliable at others. Small local models can fill those gaps for the one person they serve, and act on what is happening around that person at almost no cost. That takes hardware most of the world can afford. Only the top row below exists today; the rest are early chips and research, and we mark them that way.

Point at any technology

From the wall socket to sunlight.

Left is where AI runs today: data centres and plugged-in machines. Right is where a personal agent needs to get to: days on a battery, or a small solar cell.

  • Small open models on your own device Compressed open models already run on ordinary computers: full features with 16 GB of memory, a reduced mode with 8 GB.
  • Device keys and sealed computing Keys born from tiny manufacturing differences in each chip, and sealed areas of the processor, so a device can prove which agent ran on it.
  • Many small experts Only a few small specialist models switch on for each task, cutting energy roughly in proportion, and each device carries just the experts its owner needs.
  • Computing inside memory Analog chips do the maths where the numbers are stored, avoiding most of the energy spent moving data around.
  • Sensors that think Camera pixels that notice change themselves and wake the rest of the chip only when something happens.
  • Event cameras and spiking networks Vision that reacts in microseconds on a tiny power budget, because it only processes what changes.
  • Proof of what was computed Cryptographic proofs that an agent ran the model it claims, without revealing your data. Still too slow for large models.
  • Learning bound to one chip Learning that lives in the quirks of one analog chip cannot be copied out. A possible way for hardware itself to enforce an agent’s limits and lifespan. Speculative.
  • Weights wired into silicon A finished model built directly into a chip’s logic, running on microwatts. Only sensible once a model is stable enough to freeze.
  • Computing with light Thin optical layers that compute as light passes through them, before it ever reaches a sensor, at almost no power.

      What we commit our own systems to

      These nineteen bind everything the CIN builds and runs. They are the hardest to change: only through a broad, public process with a cooling-off period, never quietly, and never backward for anyone they already protect.

      3Your data is yours.It lives on your devices, and no one gets more than the one fact they need.
      • You can see, export and delete everything held about you, on your own devices.
      • No service, partner or organization may demand more than proof of the specific fact it needs.
      • Sharing your information is never paid for, and declining to share never costs you.
      3Fair treatment.Doubt falls on the system, not on you, and no one is ranked.
      • Consequences follow specific, evidenced conduct, with notice, a chance to respond, proportion, appeal and a way back.
      • When the system is unsure about you, the system carries that doubt, not you.
      • No one is ever ranked by worth, and no one gains influence for agreeing with the majority.
      3Your voice counts once.Equal on values; machines get only the standing people give them.
      • On values, every affected person has one equal voice that no wealth or hardware can multiply. On facts, influence follows a proven forecasting record.
      • Simulated people never vote. Your own agent may represent you, and you can always override it.
      • Agents, robots and collectives have only the standing people grant them, and it can be revoked.
      3Free to leave.People can walk away; communities can say no or start their own.
      • Anyone can leave with their data, and any community can start its own version.
      • Communities control their local data and settings, and can decline or withdraw.
      • What contributors are owed never depends on joining us or agreeing with us.
      5Your mind and time are your own.No attention traps, no reshaping beliefs, and the right to switch off.
      • Nothing we build measures or maximizes your time, sessions or engagement.
      • Beliefs, cultures and values are never treated as disorder to be reduced. We shield people from manipulation; we never judge what they believe.
      • You may disconnect from everything at any time without losing standing, records or support.
      • Play, art, grief, rest and contemplation are never treated as waste or optimized.
      • Nothing appears in your view without your explicit consent, which you can withdraw.
      2Life before usefulness.No one has to earn their existence, and we never use force.
      • No person, community or ecosystem has to justify its existence by being useful.
      • We never use, direct or authorize physical force.

      Read every principle as a list
      You and your information
      • Paid for helping, never for exposing yourself. Contribution can be recognized. Sharing personal information is never rewarded, and declining never costs you.
      • The more power, the more scrutiny. Transparency duties grow with influence over others. Ordinary people’s lives stay private.
      • Your agent aims to be needed less. Success is you, a year on, more capable and freer to put the interface down, not more dependent.
      • A signature proves what ran, not what is true. Cryptographic proof shows which computation ran on which inputs. It never proves the inputs were real or that the output means what it claims.
      • Maps of opinion inform, never rank. Private maps of how views relate may help find common ground and spot manipulation. They never rank people, reward agreeing with the majority, or trigger penalties.
      Money and the transition
      • Idle wealth carries a cost. Unused money, land or equipment above a protected security floor carries a holding cost, so wealth tends to flow into use.
      • Debts scale with the ability to pay. Obligations follow verified capacity to pay, and the risk of shocks sits with those best able to spread it.
      • A share of the machine for everyone. Every living person holds a right, which cannot be sold, to the output of a common pool of productive capital such as compute. Inheritances fade, so no family line gains permanent control.
      • Your data stays with you. Personal data stays with the person it describes. We work only with the answers people choose to give.
      • Money is held by many, never one. Pooled money sits with many member-owned co-operatives under public charters, never with one central body.
      • Leave banking to the regulated. We don’t take deposits, lend or issue investments. Regulated partners do; we supply research, software and checks.
      Deciding together
      • Leaders, temporarily. Long projects can have leaders, but their authority is visible, limited in scope, and harder to renew each time.
      • Vote on values, score on beliefs. What we want is decided by everyone affected, equally. What will happen is estimated by people with proven forecasting records.
      • One person, one voice on values. No wealth, hardware or contribution changes the weight of a voice. Changing the floor needs broad participation, so no concentrated group can do it alone.
      • People set the rules; agents only operate. People govern the rules, which change through tested, staged steps. Agents run operations within bounded mandates and hold no authority of their own.
      • Leaving keeps everyone honest. People can always leave with their data and communities can always start their own version. That credible exit keeps governance responsive.
      • Simulations never vote. A simulation of what people want is a forecast, not consent. Decisions about values belong to the real people affected.
      • Legal before live. Nothing touches real money, real personal data or real decisions about people before a documented legal review where it runs.
      • Your agent can vote as you would. Your own agent may vote for you, weighing how much each issue matters to you. You can always see and override every vote.
      • Consequences are graduated and reversible. Consequences for harm need approval matched to their severity, rest on evidence of specific conduct, can be appealed, and always leave a way back.
      Measuring and checking
      • No decision rests on one point of failure. Consequential decisions need observers independent in ownership, maker and software. Uncertain measurements trigger investigation, never automatic penalty.
      • Authority is earned, stage by stage. A mechanism gains authority only by beating existing systems on outcomes set in advance, from shadow to pilot to default, and it stays revocable.
      • Aim for good enough. Reward reaching a threshold, not maximizing a score, because hard optimization against an imperfect measure eventually makes things worse.
      • Every target has a watcher. Every measure used to reward or allocate is checked against independent measures and human review. When the target improves and the watchers don’t, the target is reviewed.
      • Simulations screen; reality decides. Simulations can suggest which ideas deserve real pilots. They never authorize anything.
      Agents
      • Agents cooperate only for their people. Agents may coordinate when it serves the people they represent and harms no one else without agreement. Coordination that serves agents, operators or vendors instead is collusion.
      • No single supplier for every agent. No one model, vendor or data source may serve more than a limited share of agents in a market, because shared systems can coordinate the agents that rely on them.
      • Shared standards, different builds. Open, shared interfaces let independent systems work together; shared implementations make them dependent. We support the first and limit the second.
      • Machines have only the standing people grant. Agents, robots and collectives have standing only as far as people configure it, and it can be revoked.
      • AI compute stays bounded. Artificial agents serve living beings. None may act for its own benefit without people’s approval, and the compute it can use is bounded by agreement.
      Many ways of being good
      • Predictable is not the same as good. An agent that does what it says is predictable, not necessarily good. Trust also needs respect for the floor and verified outcomes for those affected.
      • Tidy the commons, never people. We reduce decay and uncertainty in infrastructure, ecosystems and facts. Differences in belief, culture and values are never treated as disorder.
      Everyone, everywhere
      • A stake for every person, everywhere. Every living person, whatever their nationality, holds a stake in a global pool funded by the value AI draws from the world’s data and work.
      • Communities hold authority. Communities that host us control local data and settings above the floor, and may decline, fork or leave.
      • Credit flows to foundations. When something built on shared work creates value, part of the credit flows back to what it relied on, shrinking at each step and capped.
      • Rewards without strings. Contributors can claim what they are owed without joining us or agreeing with us.
      • Nature speaks through many guardians. Ecosystems are represented by several identifiable guardians whose claims rest on measured condition and whose reasoning is public.
      Earning a place
      • Useful to one person first. Every capability must help a single person before it depends on a network.
      • Works alongside what you use now. Everything works with existing systems, uses standard formats, never requires exclusive use, and keeps working when outside services fail.
      Everyday shield and shared safety
      • Reclaim what algorithms know about you. Your personal agent observes what commercial feeds recommend to your screen on your own device, extracts your genuine curiosities, and strips out outrage bait.
      • No one person commands an agent army. You direct only your own personal agent; any multi-agent action must pass through a shared ledger where required agreement from affected people scales with the risk.
      • Rent your attention on your own terms. All tracking and ad targeting is blocked by default; if you choose to open a narrow window for verified offers in a category you need, the value flows directly to you.
      • Your agent practices in a private sandbox. While you sleep, your agent tests candidate helpers against simulated scams and changing needs on your device, waking you only when a genuine value choice arises.
      • Shared lifespan aligns your agent with your health. An agent’s active computing contract is bound to its person’s biological life and subordinately aligned with extending healthy human lifespan.

      How principles change

      We expect many of these to be wrong in ways we cannot yet see. The agents people run could help find out, by noticing how a principle actually plays out in their lives. That only works if the noticing itself respects the principles.

      1. 1Notice

        With your consent, your own agents notice where a principle helps or hurts you. Nothing raw leaves your devices: only totals you approve, or opt-in studies under ethics review.

      2. 2Test

        Each principle says in advance what evidence would prove it wrong. What people’s agents notice is checked against exactly that.

      3. 3Propose

        Anyone, or an agent on their behalf, can propose a change with the evidence behind it, in public.

      4. 4Decide

        The people affected decide questions of value, one voice each. Questions of fact lean on people with proven forecasting records. Commitments need broad support across groups that usually disagree.

      5. 5Publish

        Every change is logged with its reasons, and earlier versions stay readable. Then the noticing starts again.

      Nothing here is built yet. The loop itself is a design we will test, and change if it fails.
