How it works

Each source is one paper, report, law, incident or release. An agent records what it says and whether it moves the world toward or away from one to three groups of our starting principles, how much it matters, and how sure the agent is. Before a source counts, our software checks it against the original: for research papers, the title must match the official record; for everything else, a passage the agent quoted must appear on the page. Sources that fail are rejected. A second agent then rates each source on its own, and where they disagree, the majority reading counts.

The eight areas

  • You and your information: whether people keep control of their own data, and whether power, not private life, is what gets scrutinised.
  • Money and the transition: whether value reaches people as AI changes work: shared stakes, debts that fit the ability to pay, money held by many rather than one.
  • Deciding together: whether people keep deciding what they want, with temporary leaders, a voice each, and the freedom to leave.
  • Measuring and checking: whether claims are checked by independent observers before anything rests on them.
  • Agents: whether AI agents stay bounded, answer to the people they serve, and do not all depend on one supplier.
  • Many ways of being good: whether systems respect differences in belief and culture instead of flattening them.
  • Everyone, everywhere: whether the benefits and the say reach people in every country, not only where AI is built.
  • Earning a place: whether new tools prove themselves useful to one person, and work alongside what people already use.

The reading for each area starts at 50, which means level. Sources toward the principles raise it and sources away lower it, weighted by importance, confidence and the kind of source: an official document or a paper counts more than a news report, and a single post counts least. A source’s weight halves every seven days, and nothing older than thirty days counts, so the reading follows what is happening now. Several reports of the same event count once.

Reading the world is a judgement, and AI agents make mistakes. Every source is linked so you can check it yourself. If one is wrong, tell us on the network site or write to contact@collectiveintelligencenetwork.org.
